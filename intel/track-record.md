## 1. 96% of our organic traffic was branded
I ran the SEO audit expecting to find which posts to write next. Instead: almost
all our search traffic was people already typing the company name. The channel
wasn't acquiring new demand — it was billing us for people we already had. Lesson:
publishing more content on a domain with no authority doesn't rank. Diagnose the
real constraint (authority, not volume) before you produce.
(Fresh — distinct from the blog-pipeline post, which is about the publishing system.)

## 2. I threw out our own battlecard before writing a comparison
We had a card calling a competitor "legacy, no AI." I checked them live before
publishing — false. They had a real, modern product. So I wrote an honest
comparison that conceded their strengths and differentiated only on what's true.
Honest comparisons get cited; dishonest ones get caught.
(Fresh — distinct from the Competitive Intel Sweep post, which is about the system.)

## 3. Cut an external stack of six vendors down to one
Paid, creative, social, execution — six outside providers, each with a slice and
no single point of coordination. Consolidated to one partner with a clear contact
per function. Fewer hand-offs beat more specialists.

## 4. A dashboard that lies is worse than no dashboard
Twice I caught our own internal tools showing a number that wasn't true — a
traffic chart "crashing" to zero that was really just reporting lag. The fix
wasn't cosmetic: I made the system fail loudly instead of quietly serving stale
data as fresh. Trust in your own numbers is the whole game.
(Use as an honest-take; keep it about the principle, not the plumbing.)
